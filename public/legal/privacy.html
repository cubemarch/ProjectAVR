AVR Capture — Privacy Policy

Last updated 21 September 2026

AVR Capture is a camera and encoder. It records video on an iPhone and sends it to a server address that you enter. It has no account, no sign-in and no directory of anything; it connects nowhere until you tell it where.

What the app collects

Nothing is collected by the developer. There is no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising identifier and no third-party SDK of any kind in the binary. No data is sent anywhere except to the destination you configure.

What stays on your device

Destinations The name, address, port and encryption settings of each server you add. Stored in the app's own preferences on the device.
Stream keys Stored in the iOS Keychain with kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly: available only while the device is unlocked, never synchronised to iCloud, and not included in an encrypted device backup. Never displayed after they are entered and never written to a log.
Capture settings Resolution, frame rate, bitrate, stereo mode and similar preferences.
Diagnostic logs Written to the system log on the device. Addresses are truncated and credentials are never included. They leave the device only if you export them yourself.

Deleting the app removes all of it, including the Keychain entries.

What is sent to your destination

When you start a broadcast the app opens one connection to the address you configured and sends:

  • the encoded video;
  • the encoded sound, if Sound is on in Settings — with Sound off the app attaches no microphone at all, so there is nothing to send;
  • the broadcast name you typed;
  • the generic device type reported by iOS — "iPhone", not a serial number, and not an identifier that distinguishes your phone from any other;
  • technical description of the picture: resolution, frame rate, whether it is one camera or two, lens separation and field of view;
  • the stream key for that destination.

Nothing else. No contacts, no location, no photo library, no advertising identifier, no list of installed apps.

The server you send to is not operated by this app. What happens to a broadcast after it arrives — whether it is recorded, who can watch it, how long it is kept — is decided by whoever runs that server, and their privacy policy governs it, not this one. Check the address before you go on air; a broadcast goes wherever its destination points.

Permissions the app asks for

Camera To record. Video is encoded on the device and sent only to your destination. Nothing is written to your photo library.
Microphone To send sound with your broadcast. iOS asks the first time you go live with Sound on — it is on by default, because a broadcast that goes out silent is hard to notice from behind the camera. Turning Sound off in Settings detaches the microphone rather than muting it: nothing is captured and no recording indicator appears. Audio is encoded on the device and sent only to your destination, alongside the video. Nothing is recorded locally.
Local network To find and connect to a receiving server on the same Wi-Fi. iOS requires this permission for any connection to a local address.

All of them can be revoked at any time in Settings. The app asks for nothing else: no location, no contacts, no photo library, no tracking permission.

Encryption

A destination can be configured to use TLS, and the app will then refuse to connect without it. Where a server has a certificate no public authority can issue for — anything on a private address — the destination link records that exact certificate and the app accepts no other. A destination that is not configured for TLS sends in the clear and says so on the screen where you select it.

Children

AVR Capture is a professional tool and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
